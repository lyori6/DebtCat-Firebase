# Legal constraints for engineering

Updated: 2026-09-17. This is an engineering policy, not legal advice or a determination that any implementation complies with every jurisdiction's law.

## Operating boundary

DebtCat is a free self-help tool for understanding debt collection notices. It is not a law firm and does not provide legal advice.

Surface objective information and user-selected options. Do not recommend responses, choose a legal strategy, assess case strength, predict outcomes, generate custom legal arguments, or file on a user's behalf. Free access, nonprofit status, and disclaimers are not substitutes for counsel reviewing the actual service.

Template filling must remain driven by explicit user choices and supplied facts. It is not a blanket legal safe harbor. Preserve existing dispute-letter legal text during copy/navigation cleanups.

## Upsolve procedural correction

The U.S. Supreme Court docket for *Upsolve, Inc., et al. v. Letitia James*, No. 25-948, records that the petition for a writ of certiorari was denied on **March 30, 2026**. The petition is not unresolved.

Source: [Supreme Court docket 25-948](https://www.supremecourt.gov/search.aspx?filename=/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-948.html), checked 2026-09-17. This procedural entry does not itself establish that DebtCat's features are lawful or unlawful.

## Marketing and substantiation

Do not use “debt expert(s),” “former debt collection professionals,” “use their expertise,” “personalized advice,” lawyer-equivalence, guarantees, or credit-repair positioning to market DebtCat. Do not make accuracy claims without relevant evidence. These are conservative product guardrails, not a claim that any single word automatically determines statutory coverage.

Apply the copy audit to everything Firebase can serve: homepage variants, archives, metadata, article copies, and shared components. Legally necessary terminology in internal documentation or neutral educational material should not be removed merely because a marketing claim uses the same words.

## Limitations periods and jurisdiction-specific questions

Do not calculate or state statutes-of-limitations conclusions, recommend payment or acknowledgment, or suggest waiting out a limitations period. Whether a particular communication, payment, or acknowledgment affects rights requires jurisdiction-specific legal review. Do not turn an extracted date into a legal conclusion.

Counsel must review:

- State-by-state UPL boundaries for the actual workflow and templates.
- Any future deadline calculation, limitations-period analysis, or revival warning.
- Dispute-letter legal text and claims about its effects or required documentation.
- Credit-services laws, payment restrictions, nonprofit exemptions, and referral arrangements before related product changes.
- Existing public articles for current law, categorical legal claims, and jurisdiction-specific advice.

This cleanup retires the public chat client; it does not disable the separately hosted legacy Worker. Worker retirement requires a separate backend task.
